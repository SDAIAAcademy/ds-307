Review of Day 2

What does the semantic layer do with this request?

A request asks for boardings.boardings and trips.departed_trips by routes.route_id for one week. Both facts reach routes. What SQL does the layer generate?

Join taps to trips, then group by route
Aggregate each fact by route on its own, then join the two results on the route
Average taps per trip, then multiply by trips
Refuse: two facts cannot meet
Why. Each fact is aggregated to the shared grain first, along many-to-one paths only; the results then meet one row per route, so nothing is repeated.

Answer: B. This is the query behind the crowding panel of today's dashboard: taps per departed trip by route. If A is popular, show the compiled SQL from Lab 2.4 again: one common table expression per fact, then a join on the route.

From question to panel

A panel is a question, a governed metric, a split, a comparison and an owner

- title: Routes leaving late
  question: Q1
  metric: on_time_rate          # from the semantic model
  dimensions: [routes.route_id]
  comparison: target            # 85%, from the brief
  also: the route's level over the four weeks before
  owner: Tawfiq Al-Sahafi, control centre
Only governed metrics. A panel that cannot name a metric of the model is asking for a definition first.

The five parts map back to the course: the metric from Day 1's catalogue and Day 2's model, the owner from the catalogue entry. The checker in Lab 3.1 refuses a metric the model does not know, such as "fare_revenue" or "ridership_forecast".

Comparisons

A number alone does not say whether to act

Target

76% against 85%: below the promise.

Prior period

76% against a usual 86%: a bad week.

Peer

76% against other feeders at 77% to 90%: this route.

Pick the comparison the decision needs. A reserve bus fixes a bad week, so Q1 shows the target and the usual level.

The example is R11 in the week of 25 October, from Lab 3.2. Ask: which comparison would make you send a reserve bus? The prior period: R11 is normally at its target and was not last week. The checker in Lab 3.1 refuses a panel with no comparison.

Before publishing

Counts below ten are hidden, and the page says so

  • Complaints by subject, one week: 4 of 6 subjects have fewer than 10
  • Small counts can point to a person and move by chance
  • The threshold is in the model's extension: small_count_threshold: 10
  • Hidden values are removed from the data, not only from the chart

On the page

"fewer than 10: accessibility, card, cleanliness, driver". The subject is named; its count is not.

The Personal Data Protection Law limits personal data to what the purpose needs; a weekly count of three accessibility complaints on one route can identify a wheelchair user. Ask: why remove the value from the data rather than hide it in the chart? Because the page ships the data inside it: the Vega specification is readable by anyone who opens the source.

Where the seconds go

One query per tile: 672 queries, 24.6 seconds

for r in ROUTES:          # 12
    for d in days:        # 28
        model.value("on_time_rate", [r, d])
        model.value("boardings_per_departed_trip", [r, d])
  • Each query reads the files, plans the joins and returns one number
  • The work is the same; only the filter changes
  • The pattern has a name: N+1 queries

Measured on a laptop CPU in Lab 3.3: 24.6 seconds for 336 tiles, two queries each. Quick mode uses one week and still takes about six seconds. Ask what would happen with 30 routes. The cost grows with tiles, not with data.

Ratios in an aggregate table

Store the numerator and the denominator, never the rate

  • A week's rate is the sum of on-time departures over the sum of departed trips
  • The mean of seven daily rates gives Friday, with half the trips, a full vote
  • Week of 25 October: the mean of daily rates is off by up to 1.9 points on a route

Day 1, again

The ratio of sums from Lab 1.3, now as a rule for any table that feeds a dashboard.

From Lab 3.3, task 4: the rate rebuilt from the parts equals the governed weekly rate exactly; the mean of daily rates does not. 1.9 points is the size of a target miss on several routes, so it would change a decision.

The equality test

A faster dashboard that shows other numbers is a new dashboard

  • Compare every tile, slow against fast, before switching
  • In the first run, 16 crowding tiles differed: 0 in the slow version, empty in the fast one
  • Those route-days had no tap with a route (East, 5 to 8 October): the table must hold 0, not nothing
  • State freshness under the title: "Data to 31 October"

This happened while the lab was written: the grouped query left the taps empty where no tap had a route, and the tile-by-tile version counted 0. The equality test found it in seconds. The four days are the anomaly of Session 4, seen here from the other side.

Segment

A fault hits everyone alike; a change in demand does not

Demand

Students fall on a school holiday; adults fall in a heatwave; segments move unevenly.

Data

On 5 to 8 October, 100% of taps on East buses lost their route, for every fare type alike.

The cause. dim_vehicle shows the East validators updated from firmware 2.4 to 3.0 on 5 October.

From Lab 3.4, task 3. Ask what segment would have moved if the drop had been real: students (a school event), or one route (a road closure). Equal loss across every segment points at the equipment. The firmware column is the kind of attribute a vehicle dimension should keep.

Correct

Restated by bus, the week of 4 October is ordinary

  • Each tap kept its bus; each bus has a home route; an as-of join finds the trip
  • Validated first: on 1 to 3 October the as-of join finds the recorded trip for 100% of R09's taps
  • Restated route taps: 20,112, +2.5% on the week before
  • No route lost riders; the timetable cut is withdrawn

A restatement is a model of missing data; trust it only after it reproduces data you already have, which is why the validation comes first. The as-of join is the same idea as Day 2's route versions: the latest trip of that bus that departed before the tap.

No blame: the note names a cause and a change, not a person. Each line has a number. The test in the last line is the reconciliation test of Day 2, applied every night. The note is the evidence for the fourth course outcome.

Day 3 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Decision first

One audience, one decision, at most six panels, each with a comparison and an owner.

Fast and the same

Aggregate once, keep the parts of each ratio, prove every tile equal.

Data before demand

Drill down, segment, validate a restatement, write four lines.
